Three lanes. One engine.

Ray owns growth: where the money goes and what it returns. The web team owns the brand's home: the site, the messaging, the book. What sits between them, and what nobody is currently doing, is the creative: the man, the worlds, the feed, and the test that tells the ad account what to spend on. That is the seat. Ray owns the account. I bring him the research and the recommendation. Nothing in it touches his decisions or the agency's.

Ray
E-Commerce Growth Manager
  • Paid media. The ad account, budgets, bidding, structure, when to scale and when to cut
  • Amazon. Listings, PPC, bundles, pricing, ranking
  • TikTok Shop and the affiliate creator program
  • Dashboards and the numbers the business runs on
  • Growth strategy across channels
What Ray gets from me: a steady supply of finished creative built for his account, a worlds test that tells him what to put money behind, a written recommendation on placement and audience with every round, and the replacement ad ready before the current one wears out.
Rob
Creative, content and community
  • The creative. Every ad, Reel, carousel, static and story. The man, the worlds, the design system it all comes from
  • The worlds test. Track, Garage, City Night, run against each other, read on the first three seconds, so spending stops being a guess
  • Placement and audience recommendations to Ray, built on that research: which world gets the money, who sees it (men 45 to 62, floor at 42), where it runs (Reels and Stories first, 4:5 in feed), and when to move budget. Recommendations, not decisions. His account, his call
  • The feed. Instagram and Facebook, four to five posts a week plus stories, on a fixed rhythm, scheduled and published
  • Community. Every comment and DM answered in the brand's voice, same day. Spam cleared. Tagged content reposted
  • Creative reporting. Which world is winning, what is fatiguing, what to build next. Five lines a week
What I do not do: run the ad account, Amazon, TikTok, or the site. I build what goes in them and I tell Ray what the research says. He decides.
Web team
Brand and site
  • The brand book and the messaging spine
  • The new site, design and build
  • Landing pages, if they are in scope
  • The logo kit and the new wordmark rollout
What they get from me: creative built on their spine, not next to it. If they say "Better skin. Zero nonsense," the feed says it too. I do not rewrite messaging. I put it in rooms.

How the loop runs, week to week

01

I build

The week's creative: ad units for Ray, feed content, stories. Every ad unit lands in a shared folder, sized and named for the account, with copy attached.

02

Ray runs

He decides what goes live, at what budget, to whom. His account, his call. Each round comes with my recommendation attached: which world, which audience, which placement, and why. He takes it, adjusts it, or overrules it. If he wants a variation, a different length, a new hook, he asks and it comes back in a day.

03

We read together

One short call a week. He brings delivery and cost. I bring hook rate, hold rate and what the feed is telling us. Between us that is the whole picture.

04

I build the next one

Winners get variations before they fatigue. Losers get killed. Ray never waits on creative and never has to make it.

The rules that keep it clean

One owner per surface

Ad account: Ray. Instagram and Facebook feed and inbox: Rob. Site: web team. Nobody has two people logging in.

Creative goes through one door

Anything that carries the brand's face on social comes from the same design system, whether it runs as an ad or a post. That is what makes the account look like one company.

Data goes both ways

Ray sees what the feed is doing. I see what the ads are doing. Neither of us builds blind.

Ray decides spend, I recommend, I decide craft

Every recommendation I make on placement, audience or budget is grounded in the worlds research and the test data, and it is his to take or leave. He can kill any creative. I can push back on any brief. Alex breaks ties, and there should not be many.

The short version: Ray runs the machine, I make what goes in it and tell him what the research says about where to point it, the web team owns the house it all points at.
