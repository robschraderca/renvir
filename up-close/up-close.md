## Why it fits Renvir right now

1. It makes the product the hero and makes it look expensive. Alex's Oct 8 note that the ad bottles "look horrible" is answered by cinematography (light, water, glass, macro on the real jar), not by AI cleanup.
2. It matches the positioning Alex sent Oct 8: ingredients and what they do, premium, strong black and white imagery, a clear reason to buy the complete system. The reel ends on the three product lineup. So does ours.
3. It needs no face. Hands, a jaw, a neck. We change one shot, the smile at 22 seconds, and everything else already sits inside the standing rules.
4. It is cheap to make well. No actor, no location, no travel. One table, one light, two sets of product, half a day.

## The idea

Same grammar, our room. Their room is a white lab. Ours is black glass at six in the morning: wet black slate, black acrylic, one hard tungsten light from the side, water everywhere. The three black bottles are the only objects. The only color in the film is what comes out of them: the gray of Alpha Detox turning to white foam, the glow of Citrus Revive with light behind it, the white of Supercharger holding its peak. Where Vichy uses green we use the liquid and the light. Carbon and tungsten, nothing else.

The man is in it and never seen. Two hands, a jaw, a neck under running water, a towel on a shoulder. Vichy turns on a smile to camera. Ours turns on a hand wiping steam off the mirror and the jaw in the glass, done for the day. Then the three stand in a line.

One sound per cut. Cut on the sound, not the picture. A low slow track underneath, closer to an engine at idle than a spa. No voice in version one. Version two carries four lines in Rob's voice.

## How we shoot it

Practical first. Macro liquids are the one thing cheaper to shoot than to generate, and better. Generators get labels wrong and get the way a real drop bends light wrong.

The table: a sheet of black acrylic and a black slate tile. One hard light from the side with a snoot, black foam core to kill spill. A clear glass dish for the light through the serum shots. Spray bottle, dropper, glycerin and water so the beads hold. Tripod. Camera: iPhone at 4K 120 fps with a clip-on macro lens, or a mirrorless body with a 100mm macro. Kit under $200 one time, inside the fee.

Product and hands: two full sets. One stays dry and perfect for heroes and the lineup, one gets pumped, wet and used. Labels checked letter by letter before anything is cut. Real hands, no actor, trimmed nails, no watch, no ring. From the man's point of view or from the side, jaw and neck only.

If a plate has to be generated: only abstract shots with no label and no skin in frame, which here means the rising bubble macro and nothing else. Live per run price stated before each roll, one roll per prompt, stop after three misses and rethink. Any jar motion is Kling 3.0 Pro with the jar layer frozen from the still. Still plates are Seedream 4. Skin contact is Veo 3.1 only and never in the same frame as the jar or the marks.

Marks and safe zones: Wordmark White with the registered R top left from frame one, never fades. Oswald for supers, Inter for the small line. Keep type out of the top 14 percent and the bottom third on 9:16, nothing below the footer. The 4:5 cut comes from the same frames with the supers moved up.

Black versus white: Vichy is a lab, so white is their truth. White would make Renvir read as a pharmacy brand and would fight every piece shipped so far. If Alex wants to see both, a white tile version is a second setup on the same day, same shots, about forty five minutes.
